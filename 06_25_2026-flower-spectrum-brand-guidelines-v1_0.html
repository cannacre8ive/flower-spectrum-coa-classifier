01 Foundation

A standard for how cannabis is chosen.

Flower Spectrum replaces indica / sativa and the THC-percentage race with the one signal a person can actually trust at the counter: aroma. Ten named profiles, each with a canonical color, derived from a flower’s real lab-tested terpene panel — the same engine behind every label, menu, and guide.

What it is

A terpene-driven, aroma-led classification framework. Every flower is run through one canonical engine that weights each terpene by how loudly it actually smells, then maps that signal onto the ten-profile spectrum. The output is a color, a name, and a plain-language aroma line — consistent everywhere it appears.

What it believes

Two flowers at the same THC can be completely different experiences. The number on the jar was never the answer. Aroma is measurable, honest, and already understood by every nose that opens a jar. Flower Spectrum gives that instinct names, colors, and the rigor of a lab sheet behind it.

Where it sits

Flower Spectrum is the retail implementation layer — the system that turns terpene data into something a shopper, a budtender, and a grower all read the same way. Craft growers are the wedge: a single decision-maker, an aroma worth telling a story about, and a label that finally does the flower justice. Dispensaries follow.

02 The Wordmark

No leaf. No mascot. A name and a spectrum.

The identity is typographic. Two lockups carry it: an expressive primary wordmark set in Newsreader, and a system signature set in JetBrains Mono for tools, footers, and labels. The colored band does the rest of the talking.

Clear space & minimum size

Clear space = cap-height of the “F”
Flower Spectrum

Keep clear space on all sides equal to at least the cap-height of the wordmark’s F. Minimum legible size: 120px wide on screen, 1 inch in print. Below that, use the system signature instead.

Construction rules

  • CasePrimary wordmark is title-case. The system signature is always uppercase.
  • ColorForeground #e8e3d9 on the dark canvas; #1a1816 on paper. Never recolor the wordmark to a profile hue.
  • PairingThe band may sit directly beneath the wordmark as a rule — never woven through the letters.
Do
  • Set the primary mark in Newsreader 600, title case.
  • Let the ten-color band carry the visual energy.
  • Drop to the mono system signature at small sizes and in footers.
  • Keep the mark monochrome against its background.
Don’t
  • Add a cannabis leaf, mascot, or icon — ever.
  • Apply gradients, glows, or drop shadows to the type.
  • Recolor the wordmark to a profile hue or stretch its tracking.
  • Reach for generic “AI / tech” aesthetics or stock dispensary clichés.
03 Color

A dark, considered canvas — and ten aromas of color.

The palette has two jobs. A restrained neutral foundation holds the interface and the page; the ten profile hues are the brand’s living language. Click any swatch to copy its value.

Neutral foundation

Accent & signal

The ten aroma hues

These are the heart of the system. Each profile owns exactly one canonical color, used identically across labels, menus, education, and the web guide. They are deliberately earthy and desaturated — a sophisticated spectrum, never neon. Gas / Fuel’s amber doubles as the system’s warning color.

Color principles

The neutral canvas never competes with the hues. A profile color appears as a fill, a 6px top-rule, or a band segment — not as body text. On paper the system shifts to the warm surface #f4eee3 with #1a1816 ink, and the same ten hues print true with print-color-adjust: exact.

Pairing rules

Never set body copy in the serif or run headlines in the mono. The mono is reserved for the precise — it earns its monospace by labeling something real (a hex, a code, a measured value). One display face, one body face, one data face. No fourth.

05 The Spectrum

Ten profiles. The soul of the brand.

This is the system everything else exists to serve. Each profile is a fixed pairing of a name, a short code, a color, and a plain sensory description — drawn from the terpenes that drive it. They never change, never get reordered, and never get a new sibling.

How a classification becomes a band
Example — Gorilla Glue #4 · validated COA Spicy / Warm 19%  ·  Gas / Fuel 17% → blend name “Spicy-Gas”
  • OrderLoudest profile leads — left to right by weighted score.
  • WidthEach segment’s width is proportional to its share of the signal.
  • JoinA profile joins the band only if it scores ≥ 60% of the leader.
  • CapThree colors maximum. A solid band means one aroma dominates.
Locked decisions — do not revisit without cause

Ten profiles, not eleven — Savory / Funk was absorbed into Gas / Fuel and does not come back. Gas is the only emergent profile (it appears when caryophyllene, limonene, and myrcene/humulene land in balance, never from a single terpene). The engine weights aroma over mass — β-Farnesene is loud on a lab sheet and quiet to the nose, and is clamped accordingly. The classification is only ever as honest as the COA behind it.

06 Voice & Tone

Confident, sensory, honest. Never clinical.

We classify the smell, which is measurable. How any flower makes you feel is yours to discover.

Principles

  • Aroma firstLead with smell and flavor. It’s what we measure and what people recognize.
  • Plain“Bright lemon,” not “dominant D-limonene expression.” Name things by the nose, not the molecule.
  • HonestEffect reputations are traditional associations — say so, and hand the experience back to the reader.
  • AssuredThe system is built on real data. The voice can be calm and certain without overclaiming.

Signature lines

“Trust your nose. It’s smarter than the number.”

“The Flower Spectrum just gives them names and colors.”

“Find a smell you’re drawn to, and you’ll find flower you enjoy.”

Say
  • “Reads as bright lemon with a soft floral edge.”
  • “Traditionally linked to grounding, restful moments.” (education only, disclaimed)
  • “A solid band means one aroma leads; a split band is a blend.”
Avoid
  • “Sedative,” “energizing,” “couch-lock” — no effect claims on labels.
  • “Entourage effect” and any medical framing.
  • Indica / sativa shorthand, or leading with a THC percentage.

08 Guardrails

Compliance is part of the craft.

Oregon OLCC discipline isn’t a footnote — it shapes the voice. Aroma always leads; effects are handled carefully and only where allowed. These rules are non-negotiable across every surface.

  • LabelsZero effect claims. Aroma and flavor only, plus factual data (THC % is data, not a claim).
  • EducationHedged effect-leans permitted — framed explicitly as traditional aromatic associations, never medical, with a visible disclaimer.
  • ForbiddenNo “entourage effect” language. No medical claims. No effect-based filtering words (“energetic,” “sedative”) in dispensary-facing tools.
  • Required line“Aroma classification — not effects or medical claims” appears in every tool footer and label compliance line.
  • Data integrityNever invent terpene or COA values. Every classification traces to a real, lab-tested panel.
An honest word on “effects”

Certain aromas carry long-standing reputations — bright citrus with a lift, deep earth with a wind-down. Those are traditional associations, shaped by culture and shared experience, and they vary by person, dose, and setting. We classify the smell, which is measurable. How any flower makes you feel is yours to discover — the aroma is just the most reliable place to start.
